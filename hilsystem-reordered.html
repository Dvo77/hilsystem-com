It started with
Dr. Piper.

At 17, he stood up at graduation in Sharon, Wisconsin and said men should never sell their influence to the highest bidder. Then he spent his life as a country vet who took chickens and handshakes instead of money.

He collected everything. Benjamin Franklin stoves. Spool beds. A lifetime of things that mattered.

When he died, the family held an auction. They walked away with $1,200.

The things didn't have stories attached. So they sold for nothing.

HIL exists so that doesn't happen to your family's stuff.

— THE PROCESS —

Four Steps.
No Install. No Account.

1
📷

Pick a spot.
Take a photo.

Any shelf. Any room. Messy is fine. Point your phone at whatever you want to catalog. Circle hidden items first — any markup tool works.

How it works

You don't need to clean up first. The AI reads the photo as-is. Any phone works. iPad + Apple Pencil is great for hidden items — but your finger or markup tool works just as well.

No special app needed. If a spot doesn't fit the standard format exactly — that's fine. The grammar is built to flex.

ANY PHONE WORKS
2
🤖

Drop it into
any AI.

Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini. Paste the HL prompt. The AI reads your photo and assigns addresses to everything it sees. A piece of tape and a marker is all you need to start.

HIL Starter Prompt
"Hey. Before we do anything else — one question: Is there a space in your life where you can never find what you're looking for? A garage. A workshop. A storage room. A junk drawer that ate your house. Tell me about it. Even one sentence. Because I'm about to show you something that might actually fix it — and it starts with one piece of tape and a marker."

The full prompt turns any AI into a guide that walks you through your first address.
