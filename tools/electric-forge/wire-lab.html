WIRE LAB // Volts, Amps & Gauge

Ohm's law, voltage drop, and ampacity — for a real wire run
Power: P = V × I = W
Wire resistance (round trip): R = ρ × 2L = Ω
Voltage drop: Vdrop = I × R = V   (% of supply)
Same math, on a physical calculator
C4× — voltage × current gives you power (P = V × I)
C4× — the wire's resistance-per-foot × the round-trip length gives total resistance
C4× — current × resistance gives you the actual voltage drop
D4÷ — voltage drop divided by the supply voltage
E5% — read that division as a percentage — over ~3% and it's time to upsize the wire

Ampacity values are conservative reference figures for low-voltage/chassis-style copper wiring (aluminum derated ~78%, ~1.64× the resistance). Real installs must follow NEC Table 310.16 (or local code) and account for insulation rating, bundling, and ambient temperature — this tool is for building intuition, not for sizing an actual circuit.
