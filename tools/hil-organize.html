Kit Prefix Dictionary

Every kit has a type.

Kit IDs follow a noun-first naming standard. The prefix tells you what kind of kit it is. The name implies the contents — you don't enumerate every item. A hammer is assumed to be in a Wall Hanging Kit.

TK
Tool Kit
Physical tools grouped by task. Contains implements, not consumables.
TK-HANG-WALL · TK-REPAIR-DRAIN
SK
Supply Kit
Consumables and restockable supplies. Tracked at macro level: Full / Medium / Low / Empty.
SK-CLEAN-KITCHEN · SK-WASH-CAR
MK
Material Kit
Raw materials you cut, apply, or install. Bought by quantity and used down over time.
MK-TAPE-ALL · MK-FASTEN-HOME
CK
Care Kit
Maintenance and preservation supplies for specific surfaces, materials, or systems.
CK-DUST-HOME · CK-FLOOR-CARE
FC
Family Kit
Inherited, gifted, or legacy collections with a named owner history and provenance.
FC-CHAR-001 · FC-SEWING-GRAN
Kit Tier System

Three tiers. One framework.

Every kit exists at one or more skill tiers. The Basic tier covers 80% of what most homeowners will ever need.

TierWho It's ForWhere Tools LiveCapability Level
Tier 1 — Basic Any homeowner Kitchen drawer, one shelf in garage Hang, patch, fix, clean
Tier 2 — Advanced Serious DIYer Dedicated toolbox, workbench area Build, repair, maintain systems
Tier 3 — Expert Trade-grade operator Dedicated shop or multiple zones Manufacture, fabricate, full-trade tasks

PRO TIP: Where you keep your tools tells us your tier. Tools in the kitchen drawer = Tier 1. Dedicated workspace with multiple zones = Tier 3.
